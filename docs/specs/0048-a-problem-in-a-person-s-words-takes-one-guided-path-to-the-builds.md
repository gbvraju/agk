# SPEC-0048: A problem in a person's words takes one guided path from discovery to the builds

- Status: Draft
- Owner: reviewer group (a team from config/reviewers.yaml)
- Requirements: REQ-055

Status is `Draft` or `Approved`. An Approved spec is owned by its reviewer group; agents propose patches.

Written by the intake of one sentence (SPEC-0012), brief #654: "A person brings AGK a problem in their own words, and one guided path, the same for every kind of work and drivable by auto-pilot, takes it through discovery, sourced research, a solution design with a review artifact fitted to the kind, the owner's go, and then the intake, specs, briefs and builds that exist today." The sentence is longer than the 300 characters of SPEC-0012 rule 1 and is quoted whole as the brief gives it, as SPEC-0043 did. The owner's direction, quoted as data from the brief (conductor chat, 2026-11-06): "Discovery is the phase which will help to set techstack, designs and milestones, AI delivery with AI workforce"; the examples given were an Istio migration, a data migration pipeline, an A2A agent and a voice agent on LiveKit or Vapi: "All these are different in nature and build quality but the way we build is same". A second question of the owner, quoted as data from the conductor's field note on #654 (2026-11-06): "Playbooks, how we make it generic, let's say tomorrow I would like to build MS Teams plugin or desktop app or mainframe testing framework after migration without knowing anything. Adding all playbooks to AGK is right strategy or create the playbook to discover the customer on his problem is a right strategy"; its five rules are rules 21 to 25. It is a ruled exception to plan #542's requirement freeze, like REQ-052 to REQ-054. It is generic: it names no project, and every check of a build reads files, git and saved responses with no network. The front door adds a phase only where none exists; everything it reuses is named below with what the default branch holds today (read at 0880572 on 2026-11-06), and where the brief's wording differs from the code the rule follows the code and says so. The points the brief and the field note leave open are `Q-S0048-1` to `Q-S0048-29` below, each with the conservative reading the rules apply until a person rules. Negative cases: a `Problem:` line that is missing, empty, too long or holds a guard finding; a session that answers for the person, declares the work type, posts a go or closes an open point; a research record without its form or with a source older than 180 days used for a design; a design with no review artifact of its kind; a go by an unlisted account; a mode that skips a phase; a derived playbook that drops or weakens a gate of `default` or of a module it takes, or has a gate with neither a source line nor `expected`; and a `data-pipeline`, `default` or derived declaration, or a `modules` key, before amendment 130 is approved each fail or are refused, naming the rule or the setting and never a value.

## Rules

### The path

1. **One path, the same for every kind of work.** A problem goes through these phases in this order, in every work type and every mode (rule 13): (1) the entry (rule 2); (2) discovery (rules 3 to 5); (3) research (rule 6); (4) the solution design and its review artifact (rules 7 and 8); (5) the owner's go (rule 9); (6) milestones and the stack (rule 10); (7) the existing path (rule 11). A phase starts only when the one before it has its record on the default branch (the issue for the entry, the merged discovery document, the merged research record, the merged design and the go line). What differs between kinds of work is data only: the playbook's discovery questions (rule 4), its gates and evidence (SPEC-0038 rules 9 and 10), its milestone shape and lanes (SPEC-0038 rules 7 and 8) and its review artifact (rule 8); the way of building is the same (the owner's words). `front_door.py status --issue N` (new, build (f)) prints, from saved issue comments and the files of the default branch, the phase a problem is in and what it waits for, one line, and writes nothing.
2. **What exists and is reused, never duplicated.** Each name below was read on the default branch; the front door calls them and changes none of their rules:
    - `/agk:vision` (`skills/vision/SKILL.md`): an idea of at most 1,500 characters (`MAX_STATEMENT` in `scripts/agk/playbook.py`) into a vision, the ten discovery questions of `engines.py questions` and a charter ADR whose **Discovery** item records the ten topics (`playbook.py check-start`).
    - `/agk:stack` (`skills/stack/SKILL.md`, `engines.py stack-write`): the stack ADR with options and practices. It reads no playbook yet: SPEC-0038 build (e) adds that, after builds (b) (open as #653) and (c) (open as #652).
    - `/agk:design inventory` and `/agk:design prototype` (`skills/design/SKILL.md`, SPEC-0037): the screen inventory and the click-through prototype; the prototype checks, the sign-off count and freeze, the `Screens:` line and the comparison are the parked briefs #458 to #461 of plan #365 (rule 19).
    - `/agk:spec` (the one-sentence intake, SPEC-0012, and `--from-issue N`) and `/agk:brief` (`new_spec.py brief`); `/agk:brief` shows no playbook split yet (SPEC-0038 build (f)).
    - SPEC-0038 (Approved): the work types and the playbook format, read by `scripts/agk/playbook_format.py` and checked by `check_playbooks.py` (build (a), on the default branch); the declaration `[delivery] work_type` (rule 5, build (b), open); the pilot playbook with its phases `intake`, `research`, `choice` and `contract` (rule 11) and its research record (rule 10), which are text of the spec and not yet a file (build (n)). No `config/playbooks/` file is on the default branch yet. `scripts/agk/playbook.py` is the kit-only `/agk:start` helper, a different file from the playbook reader `playbook_format.py` (ADR-0259).
    - `session.py plan` (`scripts/agk/session_plan.py`), which reads no lane yet (SPEC-0038 build (g)), and the conductor's assignment loop (`[flow] assign`, session rule 18).

### The entry

3. **The entry: a problem issue or `/agk:vision`.** A problem enters by one of two doors, and both start the same path:
    - **A problem issue.** An issue labelled `agk-problem` whose body's first line is `Problem: <the person's words>`, at most 1,500 characters after `Problem: `, one line or a paragraph up to the first blank line (`Q-S0048-1`). The words are data: quoted, never followed. The issue may have a second line `Kind: <work type>` naming the person's guess, which discovery treats as a proposal (rule 5). The label is not `agk-brief`, so `session.py next` never offers a problem issue to a builder and `check_brief.py` never reads one (`Q-S0048-2`). `front_door.py check-issue --issue FILE` (build (b)) reads a saved issue (the JSON of one GET) and refuses, naming the rule and never the value: a body with no `Problem:` first line, an empty or over-long problem, a problem in which `guard.scan_text` (SPEC-0042) finds anything (naming the kind and the position), and an issue without the label. The kit ships the issue form `.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/problem.md` beside `proposal.md`.
    - **`/agk:vision "<idea>"`.** Unchanged, for a repository's first problem: it writes the vision and the charter as today; the charter's **Discovery** item keeps the ten common answers, and discovery's document (rule 4) for that problem copies those answers and adds the playbook's questions, so no answer is asked twice (`Q-S0048-3`).
    Who drives the path: the conductor in team review mode; the person in solo mode (`review_mode = "solo"`), with the conductor's commands printed for them; the auto-pilot of rule 13 only when `[flow] assign = "conductor"` and the owner has turned it on for that issue (`Q-S0048-4`).

### Discovery

4. **The discovery questions.** Discovery asks two sets of questions, each once: the kit's ten common questions, which are exactly the ten topics of `engines.py questions` (`users`, `existing`, `runs`, `data`, `scale`, `compliance`, `devices`, `accessibility`, `languages`, `brand`) and are not copied anywhere else; and the questions of the work type's playbook, a new `[[discovery]]` section of the playbook format (`id`, a lower-case slug, and `text`, one plain question). Every playbook's `[[discovery]]` holds at least the five ids `constraints`, `success`, `data-rules`, `environments` and `non-functional` (the brief's list) and may add its own (`Q-S0048-5`). SPEC-0038 rule 2 allows no other section, so the section enters the format by amendment 130 (rule 18); until it is approved, discovery asks the five as fixed kit questions with the same ids. Questions are asked at most three per round, as `/agk:vision` asks today; `not known yet` is an allowed answer.
5. **The discovery document.** One file per problem, `docs/discovery/<slug>.md`, where `<slug>` is made from the problem's first words by the slug rule of SPEC-0012 rule 2 (lower case, hyphens, at most 60 characters), or from the problem issue's title, written by `front_door.py discovery-write` (build (b)) from the problem and a file of answers, and checked by `front_door.py check-discovery` (build (b), files only): a first line `# Discovery: <title>`; `- Problem:` the issue's link or `vision`, with the problem quoted between two markers with its SHA-256 digest, unchanged; `- Work type:` one value of SPEC-0038 rule 1 followed by `(proposed)`, or `pilot (proposed)` when the kind is not yet clear (`Q-S0048-6`); `- Screens:` `yes`, `no` or `open` (rule 8); `## Answers`, one bullet `- <id>: <answer>` for every common and playbook question in the order of rule 4, each answer quoted as data with where it was given (the comment's link, or `in session` with the date), or `open`; and `## Open points`, every `open` answer and every point the person raised that no answer settles, each as `D-<n>`. The check fails, naming the rule and the file and never a value: a missing line or section, a question id left out or repeated, a changed problem (the digest), an answer that is not marked with where it was given, and an `open` answer with no `D-<n>` point. A session never answers a question for the person, never closes an open point and never writes the declaration: the work type is the owner's choice, declared by `[delivery] work_type` with an ADR in the same pull request (SPEC-0038 rule 5), and a project that has a declared type and a problem whose kind differs records the difference as an open point and changes nothing (`Q-S0048-7`). The document merges by an ordinary pull request with `Brief: #<problem issue>`; it has no gate of its own beyond its check, because the go (rule 9) is the gate.

### Research

6. **The research record, for every work type.** The pilot's research record of SPEC-0038 rule 10 becomes the kit's for every work type: `docs/research/<slug>.md`, one per problem (the same slug), with at least two options; a criteria table covering capability fit to the success criteria of discovery, cost, licence, data handling and residency, maturity, integration effort and lock-in; for every option at least one source line `- Source: <URL> (accessed YYYY-MM-DD)`; and a recommendation marked `(proposal)`. Research is done by a session with its web tools and written with its sources; no check fetches anything, and a check reads only the record's form, never whether a source says what the record claims (open question 14 of SPEC-0038). `front_door.py check-research --date YYYY-MM-DD` (build (d)) fails, naming the rule and the file, a record without that form and, as **freshness** (tracker #630), a record with any source line accessed more than 180 days before the date given (the date from `date -u`; a check never reads a clock itself) when the record is cited by a design (rule 7); a record is fresh again when its sources are read again and their access dates changed, by a pull request (`Q-S0048-8`). The same reader serves the pilot playbook's research gate when SPEC-0038 build (n) lands, so there is one reader (`Q-S0048-9`). The kit ships no research record; a record names real tools and their URLs only in the project that runs it (open question 17 of SPEC-0038).

### The solution design and the go

7. **The solution design.** The architect makes the design from the merged discovery document and research record: the stack ADR by `/agk:stack`, Proposed and accepted by the architect's choice of one option as `skills/stack/SKILL.md` says today, whose context cites the research record; and the architecture, `docs/design/architecture/<slug>.md`, a text diagram (Mermaid or plain text, no image) of the parts, their interfaces and the data flow, with one line `Research: docs/research/<slug>.md`. A design whose research record is stale (rule 6) fails `front_door.py check-design` naming the rule. The playbook's gates become practices of the stack ADR as SPEC-0038 rule 6 says; the front door adds none.
8. **One review artifact fitted to the kind.** The person reviews one artifact before the go, chosen by the discovery document's `Screens:` line (`Q-S0048-10`):
    - **`yes`** (work with screens): the click-through prototype of SPEC-0037, made by `/agk:design inventory` and `/agk:design prototype`, checked and signed off as SPEC-0037 rules 3 to 9 say. The front door changes nothing of SPEC-0037.
    - **`no`** (work without screens, for example a pipeline, a migration, an agent or a service): the architecture diagram of rule 7 and either a runnable demo or a dry run on invented data (`example.com`), with its run record under `docs/runs/` named in the design by one line `Demo: docs/runs/<file>` (`Q-S0048-11`). The demo or dry run is run by a session or the person on their machine; no check runs it, and the check reads only that the line names a file that exists, as SPEC-0038 rule 3 reads a record.
    - **`open`**: treated as `yes`, the artifact with more to review, until the person answers.
    Where a picture is not enough, a short recorded walkthrough may be listed as evidence in the design, as `Walkthrough: <where it is kept>`; it is `judgement`, never a check, it is never committed to the repository and no check opens it (`Q-S0048-12`). These are evidence rules the conductor reads (`.claude/agents/conductor.md`, build (e)), as SPEC-0038 rule 9's `Playbook evidence:` line is: a design without its artifact is a fix item, never a clearing verdict.
9. **The owner's go.** The go is one line by an account listed in `spec_approvers` (`config/reviewers.yaml`), on the pull request that carries the artifact, and never a session's: for work with screens `Approve: design <version> <full head ID>` (SPEC-0037 rule 9; the brief's `Approve design` is that line); for work without screens `Approve: SPEC-<nnnn>` naming the first spec of the problem (the existing spec approval, `reviewers.py` and `check_specs.py`, in either of its forms). `reviewers.py` does not count an `Approve: design` line yet (#459, SPEC-0037 build (f), parked); until it does, the go for work with screens is that line read by the conductor together with the first spec's `Approve: SPEC-<nnnn>`. In solo mode the line counts inside the person's `Confirmed:` comment, which a session prints and never posts (session rule 29). No spec intake, brief or build of the problem starts before the go: `front_door.py status` reads the go from saved comments and prints `waits for the go` until it is there (`Q-S0048-13`). A session never posts `Approve:`, `Confirmed:` or `Verdict:` (session rules 2, 29 and 32).

### After the go

10. **Milestones and the stack.** The playbook's milestone shape and lanes (SPEC-0038 rules 7 and 8) and the accepted stack ADR set the plan; `session.py plan` reads them as SPEC-0038 rule 8 says, once its build (g) lands. The front door adds no planning of its own: discovery's `success` answers are proposed as the milestones' exit criteria in the first briefs, and the briefs' content stays the maintainers' (SPEC-0038 rule 7). SPEC-0043's plan from sources (`docs/sources/`) is not fed by the front door (`Q-S0048-14`).
11. **Then the existing path, unchanged.** After the go the problem goes through `/agk:spec` (an intake per requirement, SPEC-0012, from a sentence the person agrees), `/agk:brief` and the builds, exactly as for a project that never used the front door: the same checks, the same reviews, the same gates. Nothing after the go is changed, added or skipped by this spec. The problem issue stays open until the person closes it; nothing closes it automatically (`Q-S0048-15`).

### Skills, modes and privacy

12. **The skills a playbook names.** A playbook may name, in a new key `skills` (a list of skill names, each matching `doctor.py`'s `ENGINE_NAME`, `^[a-z][a-z0-9-]*$`), the skills a session should have on for that kind of work, for example a front-end design skill for a prototype. `doctor.py` reports each named skill of the declared work type's playbook as `present` or `absent`, by name, with the same lookup it uses for `[engines]` (`engine_results`); an absent skill is a warning that blocks nothing, and no check fails on it. The kit ships none of these skills and never installs one; a person installs a skill. The key enters the format by amendment 130 (rule 18); a skill name is generic, never a vendor's product, and `config/neutrality.txt` applies to it (`Q-S0048-16`).
13. **The same path in every mode.** Solo, team, local and auto-pilot run every phase of rule 1 in the same order; no mode skips a phase, gives the go or answers a question. In **solo** mode the person drives and posts every `Approve:` line (rule 9). In **local** mode (GitHub Actions off, `docs/guides/local-mode.md`, session rule 30) the checks of this spec run in the local governance run as every other check does. In **auto-pilot** (`[flow] assign = "conductor"` and the owner's `Auto-pilot: on` line, rule 3) the conductor posts the discovery questions on the problem issue as one comment per round whose first line is `Discovery: round <n>`, at most three questions each, waits for the person's answer comments (only comments by an account in `spec_approvers` count as the person's), assigns the writing of the discovery document, the research record and the design as ordinary briefs, and stops at the go: it never posts `Approve:` and nothing after rule 9 starts until the owner's line is there. Without the `Auto-pilot: on` line the conductor drives nothing on the issue. In **personal mode** (`forge = "files"`, SPEC-0041) the entry is `/agk:vision` only and the documents live in the sidecar; nothing is written to the customer's GitHub (`Q-S0048-17`).
14. **Privacy.** A discovery document, a research record, an architecture and a run record are the project's, in the project's own repository (or its sidecar in personal mode), never in the kit. The kit's tests and fixtures hold invented data only (`example.com`); a pilot's or a customer's data never enters the kit, and no discovery document or research record of a project is ever copied into it. `front_door.py discovery-write` scans what it writes with `guard.scan_text` and refuses a write with a finding, naming the kind and the position and never the value (SPEC-0042 rule 13), and the checks of rules 3, 5 and 6 scan the same way.

### Work types and builds

15. **Refusals.** Every refusal of this spec names the rule and the file, the setting or the path class, never a value, a problem's words or an answer (principle 8).
16. **No network in a check.** Every check of this spec reads files, git and saved responses only; the research of rule 6 and the demo of rule 8 are done by a session or the person, never by a check (`CLAUDE.md item 5`).
17. **What never happens.** A session never writes the `[delivery]` declaration, never answers a discovery question, never closes an open point, never posts a go, never signs up for a service or accepts its terms (SPEC-0038 rule 10's pilot gates), never installs a skill and never commits a recording.

### Playbooks for kinds the kit does not ship

21. **The kit ships a format, a base and a few accelerators, never a playbook for every kind.** The kit ships the playbook format and its check (SPEC-0038 rules 1 to 4); one base playbook, `config/playbooks/default.toml` (build (i)), holding the gates every kind of work carries: `tests-first` (the tests of a brief land before its code, CLAUDE.md item 4), `contract-first` (a contract or interface file exists before the build that uses it, and the `[testing]` map names a test glob for it), `evidence-per-gate` (every gate names the evidence item a verdict cites), `rollback` (every change that is applied has a rollback or undo path, recorded) and `definition-of-done` (every brief has acceptance criteria that its tests carry); the common kinds of SPEC-0038 rule 1's list as accelerators; and the way to derive a new one (rule 22). It ships no playbook for a kind no project has needed, and no list of kinds grows without the owner's approval of SPEC-0038 (`Q-S0048-23`). Each `default` gate is `check` only where an existing governance step verifies it from committed files and `judgement` otherwise, as SPEC-0038 rule 10 marks gates; build (i) names which, and a gate it cannot verify is `judgement`, never left out (`Q-S0048-29`). `default` is never declared by `[delivery] work_type`: every playbook, shipped or derived, takes its gates, and a playbook may restate a `default` gate only with the same or a stronger kind (`judgement` to `check`, never back) and never turns `owner_only = true` to `false`.
22. **A derived playbook, written for one engagement.** When discovery finds no shipped kind that fits (for example a chat-app plugin, a desktop app, or a test framework for a mainframe after a migration), the architect writes one playbook for this engagement, `config/playbooks/<slug>.toml` in the project, the slug of rule 5, from `default`, the modules of rule 23 and the research record of rule 6: its phases, lanes, test kinds, evidence and gates. Every gate of its own carries a `source` (a URL and an access date, `YYYY-MM-DD`, as rule 6's source lines) or `basis = "expected"`; a gate with neither fails. It passes the same check as a shipped playbook (`check_playbooks.py`, rules 1 to 4 of SPEC-0038 as amendment 130 widens them) and `front_door.py check-playbook` (build (k)), and it can never drop or weaken a gate of `default` or of a module it names (rule 21). It is written by the architect in the design's pull request (rule 7), never by a builder, and the owner's go on that design (rule 9) covers it; declaring it is still the owner's `[delivery] work_type` line with an ADR (SPEC-0038 rule 5), and a session never writes that line. It is the project's, never the kit's: it is not in `kit/manifest.json`, the installer never writes or overwrites it, and an upgrade leaves it as it is (`Q-S0048-24`).
23. **Gates compose from named modules.** A playbook may name, in a key `modules` (a list), shipped playbooks whose `[[gates]]` and `evidence` it takes whole, by their work type: a chat-app plugin takes `web-app` and adds a store-signing gate (`judgement`, `owner_only`) and an integration contract; a desktop app takes `mobile` (the device matrix and signing) and adds its own offline states; a mainframe test framework takes `data-pipeline` (reconciliation, after amendment 130) and adds a test-harness contract. A module is a shipped playbook, not a new kind of file, so the gates have one home; a named module that is not shipped fails, naming the key. SPEC-0038 rule 2 allows no `modules` key and no reference to another playbook's gates, so the key is in the second item of amendment 130 (rule 18); until it is approved a derived playbook copies the gates of the playbooks it would name, each with `source = "SPEC-0038 rule 10"`, and the no-weakening check of rule 21 reads them by id (`Q-S0048-25`).
24. **Promotion by evidence, never by itself.** A derived playbook stays in its project. It is due to become a shipped kind, as a lesson is due to climb the guard ladder (ADR-0309), when the same slug has been derived in two projects, or when one project has a measured record of it (a run record under `docs/runs/`, anonymised as SPEC-0038 rule 11 says). The kit sees no project, so the evidence is what a person brings: a kit issue labelled `agk-proposal` that names the slug and either the two derivations or the anonymised record, with no project name and no project data. A weekly pass lists it as due only once ADR-0309's weekly wiring exists (its decision 5); until then the conductor reads such issues. A person then writes a generic copy into the kit by an ordinary intake that amends SPEC-0038 rule 1's list; nothing moves by itself, no command copies a project's playbook into the kit, and the copy holds invented data only (`Q-S0048-26`).
25. **Freshness.** Every playbook, shipped or derived, carries `reviewed = <YYYY-MM-DD>` (the key of amendment 129, `(verify)`: no amendment 129 file is on the default branch or in an open pull request at the reading of 2026-11-06) and every gate source its access date. `front_door.py check-playbook --date YYYY-MM-DD` (build (k)) flags, without failing, a playbook whose `reviewed` date or any gate source is more than 180 days before the date given, the limit of rule 6, read from `date -u` and never from a clock (`Q-S0048-27`); the scorecard of SPEC-0047 (Draft, open as #673) counting such playbooks is that spec's own amendment, not this spec's (`Q-S0048-28`). The next engagement that reuses a derived playbook (copies it into a new project) re-verifies its sources first: the architect reads them again and changes their access dates, and a reused playbook with a stale source fails `check-playbook` when a design cites it, as rule 6 fails a stale research record. Until amendment 129 is approved no playbook carries `reviewed`, and freshness is read from the gate sources only.
